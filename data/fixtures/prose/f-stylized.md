The house was quiet — too quiet, the kind of quiet that has weight. Rain on the skylight. A kettle ticking as it cooled. Somewhere upstairs a floorboard settled, and she held her breath and waited for another that never came.

She had packed twice. Once in anger — shirts balled up, shoes loose in the bottom of the bag — and once properly, folding each thing the way her mother had taught her, as if care could make the leaving smaller. It didn't. It made it slower.

At the door she stopped. Keys. Coat. The letter, unopened, still on the hall table where he'd left it. She picked it up, put it down, picked it up again.
