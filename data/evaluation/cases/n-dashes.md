My grandmother kept everything — string, jam jars, the bus tickets from a trip to Whitby in 1962 — in a biscuit tin under the stairs. When she died we opened it expecting nothing much. What we found was a record of a life measured in small things: a pressed daisy, a receipt for a bicycle, a letter from my grandfather that began "Dear Miss Holt" and ended — two pages later — "yours, whether you like it or not."

We kept the tin. We kept the letter. The bus tickets went back in, exactly as she had left them — folded, in order, held with a rubber band gone soft with age.
