The trial suggests, but does not show, that the drug reduces migraine frequency in adults under 50. In older participants the effect was smaller and not statistically significant, which may reflect the smaller number of older participants rather than a real difference.

The authors are careful about what the trial can support. It ran for only 12 weeks, 14% of participants dropped out, mostly because of nausea, and the company that makes the drug paid for the study. They describe the result as a reason for a larger, longer trial, not as evidence that doctors should change what they prescribe.
