I built my first workbench out of construction lumber, and I'd still recommend that to anyone starting out. It's cheap. It's forgiving. And if you get it wrong — I did, twice — you've lost a weekend and forty pounds of pine, not a month and a slab of oak.

The top is three layers of two-by-fours, glued face to face. I didn't own a jointer, so I flattened each board with a hand plane on the garage floor. That took most of a Saturday. My shoulders didn't forgive me until Tuesday.

Legs were easier. Four posts, a stretcher all the way round, drawbored mortise and tenons because I'd just watched a video about drawboring and wanted an excuse. Did it need them? Probably not. But the base hasn't racked in six years, and I've pounded on that bench with everything I own.

What I got wrong: height. I built it to the height my book suggested, which was a couple of inches too low for me. Planing was fine. Sawing was fine. Anything fiddly — chopping dovetails, marking out, sharpening — had me hunched over like a question mark. I cut the legs down, then realised I'd gone the wrong way, then added blocks under the feet. It's ugly. It works.

If I built it again I'd make it taller, and I'd put the vise on the other end, where my window is. Light matters more than you think. Most mistakes I make at the bench are ones I simply didn't see.
