My workshop is a single garage, and for years it was a mess I apologised for. Tools on every surface. Offcuts in buckets. I'd spend ten minutes looking for a square before every cut — then buy another square, which didn't help.

What fixed it wasn't a big clear-out. It was a French cleat wall. One strip of plywood cut at forty-five degrees, screwed to the studs, and every tool gets its own little holder that hangs on it. I built them one at a time, whenever a tool annoyed me by being lost. It took most of a winter. It's still not finished.

The rule I try to follow now: if I use it every week, it hangs within reach of the bench. If I use it every month, it lives on the wall. If I haven't used it in a year, it goes in a box, and if the box doesn't get opened for another year, the tool goes to someone who'll use it.

Do I still leave shavings everywhere? Of course. But I can find the square. And I've stopped apologising when people come in — mostly because they're too busy asking how I made the cleats.
