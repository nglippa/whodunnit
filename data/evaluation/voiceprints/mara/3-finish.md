I finish almost everything with oil now. Not because it's the toughest finish — it isn't — but because I can fix it. A scratched varnish top means sanding the whole thing back. A scratched oiled top means a rag, ten minutes and a cup of tea while it soaks in.

My mix is equal parts boiled linseed oil, mineral spirits and a little varnish, which is roughly what the fancy tins sell for five times the price. I wipe on a heavy coat, wait fifteen minutes, then wipe off everything I can. Everything. The first time I did it I left the excess on overnight and woke up to a sticky mess that took a card scraper and a lot of swearing to remove.

Three coats is usually enough for a shelf. A table gets five or six, a day apart, and then one more a year after that. Is it as protective as a catalysed lacquer? No. Does it look like wood instead of plastic? Yes — and that's the trade I'm making.

One warning, because it matters: oily rags can catch fire on their own as they dry. I'm not exaggerating to sound careful. I lay mine flat on the concrete outside until they're stiff, then bin them. Every time.
