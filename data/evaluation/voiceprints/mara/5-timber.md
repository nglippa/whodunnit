I buy most of my timber from a yard an hour away, and I've learned to go on a weekday morning. The racks are quiet. The staff have time to pull boards down for you. And nobody is standing behind you while you sight down a plank for twist, which — trust me — you want to do properly.

What I look for is simple. Straight grain along the edges. No cracks running in from the ends, or at least short ones I can cut off. A board that sits flat on the floor without rocking. I used to chase pretty figure; now I chase boring, stable wood, because boring wood stays where you put it.

I always buy more than the cut list says. Maybe twenty percent more. Some of that is waste from defects I didn't spot. Some of it is for the mistake I haven't made yet but will. There's nothing worse than being one board short on a Sunday evening with the yard closed until Tuesday.

Then the wood sits. It comes home, gets stickered on the rack in the garage, and waits for two weeks before I touch it. Is that superstition? A bit. But I've had boards move after a fast cut, and I'd rather lose a fortnight than a tabletop.
