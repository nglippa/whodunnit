I wanted to record the dawn sounds in the allotment, and it turned out to be more complicated than I expected. I had borrowed a recorder from the neighbourhood arts cupboard and read its instruction sheet the night before. I thought the hardest part would be getting up early.

At 5:20 on Sunday morning I reached the gate and found that the spare key didn't fit the new lock. It belonged to the previous lock, which had been replaced during the week. I waited outside until Farah arrived with her own key. By then I had missed the first stretch of birdsong I'd hoped to record. Farah had come to water her beans, not to help with my recording, but she let me in and showed me a dry place to put the equipment.

Then the other sounds turned up. A delivery van stopped nearby, somebody dragged a bin across the pavement, and my jacket rubbed against the microphone. I didn't notice the jacket noise until I listened through the headphones. Moving the recorder onto a wooden crate fixed that problem. It didn't make the van or the bin disappear from the recording.

I recorded three clips of about four minutes each. The middle clip has a blackbird that I recognised by sight, but I can't identify the other birds in it, so I shouldn't present the whole clip as a collection of named bird calls. The recordings also have traffic in them, which is part of how the allotment sounded that morning.

Back home, I realised how much modest expectations matter. I had imagined a clean recording of nothing but birdsong. What I came back with was a small record of a place waking up. Next weekend I want to check the key in advance and use a tripod. I haven't decided whether I'll go earlier. Getting there before the deliveries might help, but I don't yet know their schedule.
