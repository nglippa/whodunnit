Tried the museum's new audio guide. Took the long route. Accidentally, at first. The floor arrows are good until you reach the tapestry room, where an arrow and a patch of sunlight had a small disagreement about which way I should go.

The guide has 16 stops. I listened to nine before lunch. That is a report of my patience, not a verdict on the other seven. The explanation of the wooden printing blocks was especially good. Short, specific, and no demand that I imagine myself at a turning point in civilization.

You can read a transcript while the audio plays. Useful in the clock gallery, which contains clocks doing what clocks do. I heard most of the recording but missed a few words when the largest clock struck.

One suggestion: put the stop numbers on the room map. They are beside the objects already. I found the pottery case eventually, but the map could have saved me another respectful circuit of the tapestries.
