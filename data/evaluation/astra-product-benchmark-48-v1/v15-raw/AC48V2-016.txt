We compared the time taken to identify a misplaced label under two shelf arrangements. In arrangement A, labels were placed at the front edge of the shelf; in arrangement B, they were placed above the relevant shelf. Twelve participants each performed one search under each arrangement, and the order was alternated between participants.

The median search time was 19 seconds in arrangement A and 24 seconds in arrangement B. These results do not support a broad conclusion that one arrangement is superior. No uncertainty interval was calculated, and the sample consisted of staff who already knew the room.

The task involved a single deliberately misplaced label. Ordinary searching may involve several labels, unfamiliar categories, or interruptions. The results therefore support only a further trial. A trial with visitors would show whether the observed time difference also appears among people without prior familiarity with the shelves.
