In this exercise, groups compared two sets of fictional interview transcripts. One set included pauses marked in brackets; the other presented only spoken words. Students were asked to identify places where they wanted more context, rather than to assign a confidence score to the speaker. The exercise gave them a chance to discuss transcription choices. Several groups noticed that punctuation could imply a stronger conclusion than the recorded words supported. Others focused on how the removal of repetition changed the rhythm of a reply. These observations were collected on paper at the end of the class. They were not graded, and no attempt was made to rank the two transcript formats. A later session will examine how different transcription conventions handle overlapping speech.
