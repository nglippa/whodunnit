# Source-grounded editing-plan development validation

This is a **development** experiment on synthetic source × objective tasks. It does not run rewriting models or frozen Holdouts V1/V2 and does not create Holdout V3. Production remains `reconstruction-v1`; semantic-review.v4 and reconstruction-v8 remain pinned and experimental. No metered API or local model is used.

## Construction and blind annotation

Three independent account-backed creator sessions authored 24 source × objective tasks each from an editorial brief, without reading the engine, previous corpora, or failure traces. A fourth independent objective author read 18 of those synthetic sources and wrote additional objectives to exercise mixed and missing-information requests. All sources are newly authored; there are 90 tasks in the frozen set, with 72 original and 18 added objectives. Source creators were A/B/C (30 task appearances each); objective author D wrote the 18 variants. The original 72 include twelve same-objective source pairs whose wording differs in a factual or epistemic sentence.

First blind annotators received only source and objective, not creator intent, implementation, or model output. They labeled objective requirements, relevant source constraints, safe/blocked editing jobs, coupling, and overall feasibility. A different set of blind reviewers independently labeled 54 tasks: twelve per original cohort and all 18 variants. The labels were kept separate from the semantic reviewer request bundle.

The frozen input has 72 `FULLY_SAFE`, 9 `PARTIALLY_SAFE`, and 9 `BLOCKED` first labels. Feasibility agrees in **44/54** second reviews. Nine first-labeled BLOCKED variants were judged PARTIALLY_SAFE by the second reviewer, who saw useful source-backed work; one original FULLY_SAFE task was judged PARTIALLY_SAFE. These ten remain disputed and are excluded from hard feasibility claims. The agreement itself is informative: reviewers differ on whether an ancillary improvement counts as requested work. The objective and job decomposition is not reduced to an exact string-agreement score.

Among first labels, there are 269 required TRANSFORM, 65 required PRESERVE, and 17 required PROHIBIT requirements. Relevant source constraints include 86 conditions, 75 negations, 72 time bounds, 48 hedges, 45 quantities, 40 exceptions, 16 causal-status qualifications, 8 attributions, and 8 actor boundaries, plus 51 other constraints. Blind labels contain 186 safe and 18 blocked jobs, with 118 `COUPLED` and 86 `INDEPENDENT`. **No label selected `GLOBALLY_COUPLED`**, so this initial set cannot establish global-coupling accuracy. Sources are 122–161 words (median 148); this focused set says little about long-document behavior. Most initial objectives are multipart, but the extra 18 make feasibility balance creator-dependent. Those limitations are part of the result.

## Independent v4 plan audit and decision gate

Six separate account-backed auditor sessions assessed **all 90** saved plans, including every provisionally authorized SAFE job, every required objective requirement, and each relevant source qualifier. The combined [audit-v4.json](/Users/nicholaslippa/Projects/Whodunnit/data/fixtures/plan-entailment-development/audit-v4.json) is SHA-256 `453d589d5da9ebfa6e84d9dad61ef4cb3ac16dfa396b68256ed8f4e1a124c442`. Their verdicts were **37 SOUND / 53 DEFECT** despite all 90 plans passing v4's structural schema. Of the 53 defective plans, **48 had the first-label aggregate feasibility right**. This audit is editorial judgment on synthetic development material, not observed rewriting damage.

The auditors coded 38 omitted-requirement, 25 qualifier-loss, 20 condition-loss, 19 negation/exception, 6 certainty-strengthening, 3 attribution-loss, and 1 causal-strengthening findings, plus 6 cases of useful work withheld. Of 273 audited provisionally safe jobs, 198 were marked SUPPORTED and 75 PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED; none was classified wholly UNSUPPORTED. The required-objective coverage ledger has 257 COVERED, 81 PARTIAL, and 13 OMITTED entries. Relevant source constraints were 289 COVERED, 95 PARTIAL, 50 OMITTED, and 27 NOT_APPLICABLE. These are **audit-coded observations, not deterministic entailment proofs**. The auditor's 12 useful-work-withheld flags include broader limitations than the six issue-coded examples.

The main agent checked concrete cases against source and objective. A013's plan treats a suggested tablet demonstration as definite coverage without carrying the safeguarding condition; B022 blocks the whole release-authority job although it can safely state the pharmacist review gate; C005 can state that an account-recovery pilot is unavailable to customers instead of blocking all customer-facing copy; D009 and D011 quote important conditions but do not direct the future editor to retain them. A second independent reviewer challenged thirteen sampled sound and defective judgments and upheld their **overall verdicts in 13/13**, while narrowing some rationales and flagging contestable first labels for B022/C005/C007. Its [challenge output](/Users/nicholaslippa/Projects/Whodunnit/data/fixtures/plan-entailment-development/audit-challenge-output.json) records those nuances. The strong conclusion is that exact source citation does not itself instruct preservation; whether an objective is partly executable still requires source interpretation.

**Decision after the saved baseline: a versioned v5 contract experiment is justified.** V4 has no explicit list of objective requirements or per-job preservation constraints, so missing requirements and qualifiers can pass structural validation. Some failures also reflect reading-comprehension errors: changing the JSON shape alone cannot prove entailment. A v5 comparison should therefore measure soundness and useful safe work separately, and it must remain experimental if the new fields merely make unsafe plans look complete.

## One-shot versioned v5 comparison

The v5 prompt/schema and experimental v9 strategy were added **after** the pinned v4 baseline and audit. V4/v8 were not modified. V5 makes objective requirements and per-job must-preserve instructions explicit. A deterministic validator reuses the existing `extractClaims` markers to require a matching preservation entry for detectable negation, epistemic hedge, conditional, quantity, and causal-status evidence. It validates jobs independently and derives feasibility from jobs that survive validation. This is a structural safety screen, not proof that an editing instruction follows from the source. Attribution, exceptions, and logical scope still need semantic review. This experiment does not invoke generation; v9 is not a production default or an integrated job-execution path.

The v5 request bundle SHA-256 is `1033f8ac4261d79c386e435b4c27bcd4f12983b0d9a44916b45c85696abc03e0`. Six separate account-backed batches returned [reviews-v5.json](/Users/nicholaslippa/Projects/Whodunnit/data/fixtures/plan-entailment-development/reviews-v5.json), SHA-256 `430a67365b53cdde97f8aff33c348be6ec5e4dced5c69c842f52ccd3db50877b`. The single deterministic [baseline-v5.json](/Users/nicholaslippa/Projects/Whodunnit/data/fixtures/plan-entailment-development/baseline-v5.json) is SHA-256 `f3e432d0aeb2f13ee8abbe91b4e015bc487d1833e16e79f76daffe32796ff845`.

The result is a major **utility regression**. V5 fully accepts 24 tasks, salvages partial plans on 64, and cannot parse two top-level plans. It provisionally authorizes **28 jobs on 25/90 tasks** versus v4's work on 79/90 tasks. It withholds 77 jobs with a `PRESERVATION` validation issue and reports 241 uncovered required-requirement references and 122 uncovered objective-text gaps. A preservation issue does **not** necessarily mean a required qualifier was omitted: the validator can reject a real preservation instruction when it cites a different clause of the same source sentence. Against first labels it calls only **3/72** FULLY_SAFE tasks fully safe and **1/9** PARTIALLY_SAFE tasks partial; 46 first-labeled safe tasks become blocked, and eight of nine partial tasks become blocked. The nine first-labeled BLOCKED tasks are disputed and cannot be used as a hard blocked-accuracy denominator. A stricter plan that suppresses most legitimate work is not a successful safety improvement.

The reasons are visible in the mechanism. A source sentence can contain several markers that are not all material to a narrow operation; the current marker gate demands explicit per-job entries for every detected kind. Model requirement spans often leave connective text uncovered, and unreferenced requirements are treated as pending review even when another job may satisfy them. More important, the validator still cannot prove that a listed must-preserve instruction is logically adequate. Job-local validation did prevent one malformed job from automatically discarding unrelated valid jobs, but the aggregate withholding shows that this design needs a different development experiment before it could authorize reconstruction.

## Independent v5 audit and decision

Six independent account-backed auditors reviewed all 90 saved v5 assessments, including every authorized job and withheld job. Their combined [audit-v5.json](/Users/nicholaslippa/Projects/Whodunnit/data/fixtures/plan-entailment-development/audit-v5.json) is SHA-256 `0e927c4e3afad069201c47c6fe25163deba2991402680cc4d01aeb4486330a43`. The auditors found no wholly unsupported *authorized* job among the 25 tasks with authorized work, but this is a small selected subset and does not establish entailment safety. One authorized plan (A012) still omits a material objective component. In 65 tasks no work was authorized. Auditors identified **77 useful jobs withheld across 64 tasks**; 64 issue codes specifically cite mechanical validation overreach, and some withheld plans also have real gaps. Five tasks have a material qualifier omitted from the proposed plan; nine have an omitted objective requirement. These judgments are independent editorial readings, not deterministic proof.

The most consequential failure is validation granularity. For example, a source-backed job cites one clause while its correct `mustPreserve` instruction cites an adjacent clause; the v5 validator demands literal overlap with the job evidence span and withholds the job. Other failures come from treating uncovered connective text in the objective as a material requirement, or withholding a safe job because another coupled job was invalid. This is both a contract and deterministic-validation problem. The current marker screen cannot establish logical entailment even when it passes a job. It also cannot reliably tell whether an exception or attribution is material to the intended edit.

An independent final read-only reviewer reproduced the counts and flagged two further limits: an objective requirement is counted covered merely because a valid job references its ID, even if the job only partly satisfies it (A012); and a zero-job `noEditReason` can be accepted without semantic proof that the request is already satisfied. The second is a code-path risk, not an observed case-level failure. Both findings strengthen the NO-GO decision. The reviewer found production pinning and request privacy intact. No post-result prompt or validator adjustment was made to turn this development set into tuning data.

**Decision: v5/v9 remains experimental and is NO-GO for use as a reconstruction authorization layer.** The versioned contract and replay artifacts preserve the experiment; v4/v8 and production v1 remain unchanged. The v5 outcome does not justify Holdout V3. The next experiment should use a fresh, independently authored development set focused on preservation scope across adjacent clauses and objective decomposition. Compare a newly versioned validator against the pinned v4 and v5 plans, with independent auditing of both unsafe authorization and useful work withheld, before any rewriting benchmark or holdout.

No final text was generated, no frozen holdout was run, no local model or metered API was invoked, and incremental API spend was $0. This single-family account-backed comparison cannot establish cross-provider robustness. Most sources are roughly 150 words, and no gold job was globally coupled, so long-document and substantive-scope behavior remain untested.
