# Discourse research for a deterministic editor

Scope: public writing guidance and research only. The proposals below are editorial signals, not claims that a passage is meaningful, coherent, persuasive, or appropriate for its audience.

| Topic and source | What the source supports | Limit of the inference | Fast, deterministic editor principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nominalization and plain language — [Purdue OWL, *Avoid Common Pitfalls*](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/conciseness/avoid_common_pitfalls.html); [CDC, *Clear Writing Assessment Tool*](https://www.cdc.gov/nceh/clearwriting/docs/Clear_Writing_Assessment-508.pdf) | Action expressed as a noun plus a weak verb can make a sentence longer and obscure the action. | A noun ending is not proof of a hidden verb; a nominalization may be a necessary term or useful topic reference. Plain language depends on audience and purpose. | Flag a small, explicit list of support-verb + action-noun patterns for optional review. Require an unambiguous local rewrite before changing text; preserve the actor, object, tense, and negation. Do not ban suffixes or nominalizations wholesale. |
| Cohesion versus coherence — [Mann & Thompson, *Rhetorical Structure Theory*](https://www.sfu.ca/rst/pdfs/Mann_Thompson_1987.pdf); [Khalil, study of cohesion and coherence in student writing](https://doi.org/10.1016/0346-251X(89)90008-0) | Text parts can have functional relations; a study found that the number of cohesive ties was only weakly associated with human coherence ratings in its sample. | The study used 20 compositions in one setting. A connective, repeated term, or pronoun is observable, but its presence does not establish that claims fit together. | Count surface links only as **cohesion cues**. Surface a possibly unclear reference or missing transition as a question, never a coherence failure or an automatic connective insertion. |
| Lexical diversity and length — [Covington & McFall, *Moving-Average Type–Token Ratio*](https://doi.org/10.1080/09296171003643098); [Koizumi & In’nami, short-text length study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.system.2012.10.012) | Raw type–token ratio varies with sample length; moving-window measures reduce that dependence, while short texts still require care. | No diversity index says whether a word choice is precise, natural, or suitable. Window choice and tokenization affect the result. | If diversity is shown, use a fixed window, disclose token count and window size, and suppress the signal for text shorter than the window. Never optimize toward a universal target or replace repeated key terms merely to raise a score. |
| Information density — [study of lexical density in research abstracts](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lingua.2024.103837); [U.S. Health Literacy Online, *Write in plain language*](https://odphp.health.gov/healthliteracyonline/create-actionable-content/write-plain-language) | Content-word proportion is one measurable form of lexical density; clear public-facing guidance favors context before new information and manageable sentences. | Content-word ratio is not the amount of factual information, reader comprehension, or writing quality. Density differs by discipline, genre, and audience. Sentence-length advice is context-specific. | Label content-word ratio and long-sentence counts as descriptive **measures**. Use them to locate passages for review, without a global “more/less dense is better” score or automatic deletion of context. |
| Redundancy and necessary repetition — [Purdue OWL, *Concision*](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/conciseness/index.html); [U.S. Health Literacy Online, *Write in plain language*](https://odphp.health.gov/healthliteracyonline/create-actionable-content/write-plain-language) | Concision removes words that add no useful work; defining an unfamiliar term and supplying context can warrant extra words. | Repeated strings can serve emphasis, reference, or reader orientation. String overlap alone cannot establish that two claims are redundant. | Offer only narrow, local suggestions for exact duplicate words or established redundant phrases. Show the span and let the writer decide; do not deduplicate sentences by lexical similarity. |
| Genre and rhetorical structure — [Mann & Thompson, RST](https://www.sfu.ca/rst/pdfs/Mann_Thompson_1987.pdf); [Lund University, research-introduction moves](https://www.awelu.lu.se/writing/writing-stage/structuring-the-text/structure-of-introductions/); [engineering-field move analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amper.2014.12.002) | Discourse organization reflects relations between spans and the communicative purpose of a genre. Research introductions often use territory, niche, and contribution moves; patterns vary across fields. | A move framework for research introductions is not a universal outline. Headings or cue words cannot prove a rhetorical move is present, adequate, or correctly ordered. | Offer genre-specific, optional checklists only when the writer chooses a genre. Use explicit headings or cue phrases as navigational hints, not as evidence that an argument, gap, or contribution exists. Never reorder prose automatically from a generic template. |

## Boundary for implementation

- **Measures:** token counts, fixed-window lexical diversity, content-word proportion, sentence length, exact repetition, and explicit connective or heading occurrences. Specify tokenization, window, scope, and minimum sample size wherever a measure appears.
- **Semantic judgments:** whether two ideas actually follow, a reference has the intended antecedent, repeated content is dispensable, information is sufficient, or a passage fulfills a genre's rhetorical purpose. These require context and reader interpretation; a deterministic editor should present uncertain cases as questions or leave them alone.
- **Rewrite gate:** apply only local transformations with predictable grammar and preserved actors, claims, qualifications, and scope. Otherwise show an editable suggestion rather than silently rewriting. Evaluate suggestions separately across genres and text lengths; do not treat an increase in a surface metric as improvement by itself.
